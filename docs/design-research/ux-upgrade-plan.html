28 October 2026 · research for the EOD app

Maginhawa UX upgrade plan

A review of the live app (phone and head office desktop) against design psychology and UX research. Each change keeps the approved look: one dark card, Monzo-style lists and Things 3 circles. It changes how the screens behave, not the style.

Improvements found
17 changes

The design system is in good shape. The gains come from three places: giving the phone screen back to the form, making status and colour mean exactly one thing, and designing the end of the night (resume, submit, done) with as much care as the start.

Do first

6 · each about half a day

Small changes with the biggest effect on a tired manager at close.

1

Give the phone screen back to the form

Seen: on a 375×812 phone, the head office banner (4 lines), the sticky button pair and the tab bar take up about 45% of the height. On Today's EOD, only 1½ section rows show.

Change: shrink the "You're in Guanabana as head office" banner to one 32px line ("Viewing as head office · Exit"). Show one sticky primary button only; move the secondary text link into the page body. Hide the tab bar while the keyboard is open.

Fitts's law and Hoober's grip research: what's on screen is what gets done. Under stress, attention narrows to the centre (Easterbrook), so banners at the edges cost space and are rarely read.

FittsCue utilisationSignal over chrome
2

Make "empty" look different from "£0.00"

Seen: every empty input shows "£0.00" in grey, and the hero reads "£0.00" before anything is typed. At midnight in dim light, empty and zero look the same.

Change: leave empty inputs blank, or show a faint "—". Show "—" in the hero until the first figure is entered. Show last week's figure underneath as a hint ("Last Wed £812.40"), never inside the box.

Norman: slips happen on autopilot, and experts make the most. A value that looks entered gets skipped. Placeholders sit at about Lc 30 on APCA, which is too faint to read but loud enough to mislead.

3

One way of saving, said once

Seen: the Lunch screen shows "• Saved" at the top and a big "Save lunch" button. That's two ideas of how saving works. Does the button do something the autosave didn't?

Change: autosave stays. The button names where it goes: "Done · back to Today" (or "Continue to Dinner"). The status gets a time: "Saved 23:41", or "On this phone · sends when online".

4

Ask "What ran today" first

Seen: the Lunch, Dinner and Delivery switches sit at the very bottom of Today's EOD, below the Submit row. They decide which sections exist, but managers see the full 8 first.

Change: show them as a compact row of three chips under the hero (on by default from the site's normal hours). The section count then reads honestly, e.g. "0 of 7".

Tesler's law: the app takes on the complexity. Serial position: whatever decides scope goes first. Endowed progress: a smaller, honest total feels closer to done.

TeslerSerial positionGoal gradient
5

Make colour mean one thing everywhere

Seen: "Not started" has a green dot. The head office sales line turns red on every quiet weekday dip, so it goes red every week. "Needs attention" uses coloured dots on their own.

Change: neutral grey for "Not started". Draw the sales line in ink, with red only where a day falls outside the forecast band. Every dot in Needs attention gets a word or icon ("Over", "Short", "Late").

Von Restorff: a highlight only works when it's rare. GOV.UK: use red only for "there is a problem". WCAG 1.4.1: colour is never the only cue (about 1 in 22 people have a colour-vision deficiency).

Von RestorffWCAG 1.4.1Alarm fatigue
6

Calm the "Needs you" list on Home

Seen: at 23:09, Home shows "9 min late" in red plus three red "Missed" pills stacked. Nothing in the missed items can be done now, but they shout the loudest.

Change: one red item at a time: the thing to do now. Fold earlier misses into one neutral row ("3 missed earlier · add a note"). Show "late" in orange until there's a consequence.

Peak-end: the end of a shift shouldn't open with guilt. NN/g on alert fatigue: if everything alarms, nothing does. The aim is a manager who feels in control, not watched (Norman's reflective level).

8 · a day or two each
7

Put fields in the till report's order, with last week's figure as an anchor

Managers copy straight down the Z-report. If the screen's order matches the paper, there's no searching. Each field shows the same weekday last week in small grey text. A wrong figure then looks wrong before any check runs.

Mental models (the gulf of execution); recognition over recall; Cowan: working memory holds about 4 items, and fewer when tired.

8

Format on blur, accept anything sensible

Accept "1234.5", "£1,234.50" and "1 234,50". Reformat to "1,234.50" when the field loses focus, never while typing (it moves the cursor). Errors use GOV.UK wording, e.g. "Gross sales must be an amount in pounds, like 850.00". No "invalid", no "please".

Norman: prevent slips with forgiving formatting. GOV.UK validation pattern. Baymard: validate on blur and clear the error the moment it's fixed.

9

Plausibility checks in plain English

Extend the "It's right" warnings with a comparison: "That's 4× a normal Wednesday lunch. Check, or It's right." Show it inline under the field, in the centre of view, never as a top banner.

Slips are the main risk for experts. Under stress, peripheral warnings drop out (Easterbrook).

10

Resume exactly where they were pulled away

Managers get interrupted by the safe, a guest or a staff question. Reopening the app should land on "Pick up where you left off: Cash, Lunch", with the field focused and the scroll position restored.

Gloria Mark (CHI 2005/2008): interrupted work often isn't resumed for 20+ minutes, and people rush afterwards. Zeigarnik: a half-filled circle is a gentle pull.

11

Say how long it takes, and count what's already done

Under the title: "About 6 minutes · saved as you go". Count honest pre-fills as done (site, date, who's reporting, staff from the rota), so the bar starts at 1 of 7, not 0. Never pre-fill counted money (cash, tips).

NN/g transparency principle. Endowed progress (Nunes & Drèze: 34% vs 19% completion). Default effect, used honestly: a default on counted cash invites false confirmation.

12

Make "Day closed" the best moment of the night

This is the one place for motion: the hero circle fills to a tick (a 450ms spring with no bounce), the total settles, and the copy reads "Received by head office 23:52". No confetti. Under reduced motion, use a simple cross-fade. Every other screen stays still.

Peak-end rule: people remember the peak and the end. Emil Kowalski / NN/g: actions done 100 times a night don't animate; rare moments earn it. Nielsen: rationed celebrations stay meaningful.

13

Head office: one hero, bullet graphs, fewer range buttons

The All sites page has three dark cards; that breaks the one-hero rule, and all three compete. Keep the group net as the hero. Show "vs forecast" as a bullet graph (actual bar, target tick, band). Cut the 8 range buttons (1D…All) to 1W, 1M and 1Y plus a "More" menu. Sort the sites table with exceptions first.

Hick's law; Stephen Few: bullet graphs over gauges; NN/g: use position and length for amounts, colour only for exceptions.

14

Keep the focused field visible above the keyboard and sticky bar

Add scroll-padding-bottom equal to the sticky bar's height, drop the safe-area padding while the keyboard is open (an iOS WebKit bug), and use dvh rather than vh. Test on a real iPhone and an older Android.

WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.11 (focus not hidden). WebKit bug 292603.

15

One prompt at each site's own close time

A single push notification when the site actually closes, e.g. "Till cashed up? Today's EOD takes about 6 minutes". Nothing else gets pushed; the rest goes in the 07:00 digest.

Fogg behaviour model: behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a prompt line up. At midnight motivation is low, so the prompt has to land at the right moment and the task has to be easy.

16

A dark mode legibility pass

NN/g finds light mode more legible, especially for small text and for people with astigmatism. Keep following the system setting, but check that every caption in dark mode reaches APCA Lc 60 or more and that no weight below 500 is used under 14px.

NN/g dark mode study; APCA draft guidance; Material's rules for dark surfaces.

17

Test at a real close

Sit with 3–5 managers at two sites at actual close time. Measure time to submit, slips caught on Review, and where they hesitate. A good look earns forgiveness for small problems but not for slow ones, and it can hide them in demos.

Aesthetic-usability effect (Kurosu & Kashimura). Samsara / Toast: frontline tools need field research, not just remote tests.

Popular ideas to avoid leaning on

Weak evidence

"7±2 items"

Miller's number was partly rhetorical. Cowan puts working memory at about 4 chunks. Don't use 7 as a design limit.

Decision fatigue and ego depletion

A 23-lab replication found effectively no effect (d = 0.04). The robust point is simpler: tired people slip more.

Choice overload

A 2010 meta-analysis of 50 experiments found an average effect of about zero. Keep lists short because of Hick's law, not "paralysis".

Thumb-zone heat maps

Hoober later corrected his own drawings: people favour the centre, and corners are least accurate. Treat the maps as a guide, not a rule.

Streaks and variable rewards

Broken streaks demotivate (Silverman & Barasch 2023), and "Hooked" mechanics feel like surveillance in a work duty. Keep "on time X of Y".

Zeigarnik effect

Replications are mixed. Use it for gentle resume cues, never for nagging.
