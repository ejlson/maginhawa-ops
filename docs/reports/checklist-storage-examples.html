How checklists are saved, found and shared

Staff tick off small checks on a phone or iPad. The app saves every answer once, to SharePoint. Everyone else sees the same answers in the form that suits them.

3 · What managers find in SharePoint

A folder for each restaurant, filled by the app, never by hand. After closing, each day becomes one PDF holding all of that day's checklists, and each month gets one Excel file. Missed days get a PDF too, so a gap is never just a missing file. Staff can open them but not change them. Photos sit in their own library, filed by day and checklist.

5 · Why not keep it all in Excel or in files?

We looked at four ways to store the checklists. Only the last one is safe when several staff save at once and is still easy for people to read.

One Excel spreadsheet
  • Microsoft doesn't let an app like ours add rows to Excel on its own
  • Locks and clashes when two people save
  • No safe record of changes
Doesn't work
A file per checklist only
  • Easy to browse
  • Can't search across restaurants or days
  • Clashes when people save at once
Fragile
SharePoint lists only
  • Safe, fast to search, tracks changes
  • Looks like a spreadsheet, but harder for managers to browse on a phone
Safe, less friendly
Lists, plus files made for you
  • The lists keep the record safe
  • The app makes PDFs, monthly Excel files and inspection packs
  • Nobody types anything twice
Recommended

Sources: Microsoft Graph documentation (adding rows to an Excel table isn't supported for an app working on its own), SharePoint limits, FSA Safer Food Better Business guidance. See the full report for details.
