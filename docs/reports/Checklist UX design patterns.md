# Build checklists that keep honest records

Researched 9 November 2026. Sources are mostly vendor help centres, UK regulator and council documents, and design-system guidance. Vendor speed claims are self-reported. Note: `help.safetyculture.com` pages now redirect to `help.mitti.com`; the product is called SafetyCulture throughout.

The best operational checklist apps share one shape, and Maginhawa should copy it. Staff work through a **scrollable list grouped by time slot (opening, mid-shift, closing), answering items in any order with one tap**. Out-of-range temperatures open an inline corrective-action panel. A single Submit lists exactly what is still outstanding. Managers build templates on desktop in a structured editor that **autosaves a draft and only goes live on an explicit Publish**, and published changes apply to new runs only. Submitted records are **never overwritten**. A correction becomes a new version with the original still visible, a named editor, a timestamp and a mandatory reason. Late entries stay visibly late, because UK councils have prosecuted businesses for back-filled temperature logs. UK food guidance (the FSA's Safer Food, Better Business and MyHACCP) says what records must show but says almost nothing about how to correct digital records. The MHRA's data-integrity guidance, written for medicines, is the strictest available UK benchmark and is the one to design to. On the visual side, the numbers converge: **44 px minimum hit areas, 48–56 px rows for kitchen use, a 4/8 px spacing scale, 17 px body text, tabular numerals, and status shown by colour plus shape plus word**. The main gaps are independent evidence on gloved and wet-finger touch accuracy, glare in bright kitchens, and any vendor's documented "central template plus local extras" model. Maginhawa will have to design those parts from first principles.

## Daily checks belong in a list, not a wizard

Every restaurant checklist product with documentation presents **a list of items grouped into lists, sections or pages, with exceptions handled per item and one Done or Submit at the end**. None documents a one-question-per-screen stepper for routine checks. Jolt staff pick a list, tap into each item to record it, then tap submit ([Jolt help](https://help.smartsense.co/en/articles/9654164-how-to-record-temperatures-using-the-temperature-probe-in-the-jolt-app)). Trail staff tick or flag each item and select "Mark as done" ([Trail starter guide](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/1004951-starter-guide-for-teams)). Lumiform explicitly lets auditors **answer out of sequence while walking an area** ([Lumiform](https://lumiformapp.com/app-uses/osha-compliance-software)). The research backs this up. NN/g says wizards suit novice, infrequent or branching tasks, become tedious when repeated, are "not easily interrupted", and are wrong "if users benefit from completing steps in any order" ([NN/g: Wizards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/)). Opening and closing checks are the opposite case: done daily by the same people, in whatever order they physically walk the kitchen, and interrupted constantly.

A focused one-at-a-time view still has a place. It suits new starters, rarely run checks such as a monthly deep clean, and the corrective-action branch after a failure. A sensible hybrid keeps the list as home and lets a tapped item open a focused card with large controls and a "Next unanswered" button, close to Jolt's tap-into-item model. One warning comes from GOV.UK. Accordions that show or hide whole sections conditionally disoriented users, and removing a progress indicator made **no difference to completion** ([GOV.UK user research](https://userresearch.blog.gov.uk/2015/08/13/no-more-accordions-how-to-choose-a-form-structure/)). A progress bar alone therefore adds little. An interrupted chef needs a count of what is left ("4 left · 1 needs action") and the specific outstanding items.

Products enforce completion with blocking rules. Trail refuses to save a task until every item is ticked or flagged, marks mandatory record-log fields with a red asterisk, and can demand a comment or photo ([Trail: Why can't I submit](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/3690180-why-can-t-i-submit-my-task)). Lumiform blocks completion until required questions are answered ([Lumiform](https://help.lumiformapp.com/en/knowledge/mark-a-question-as-required)). Trail's warning appears only when Done is pressed, which is a late-error pattern. The better version shows the outstanding list above the Submit button, or disables Submit with the reason stated. Baymard's finding that inline validation **does not replace a good error summary on submit** supports showing both ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). Trail also separates states that matter for reporting. Its flag offers **"Can't complete" and "Not applicable"**, managers get "Never applicable" to hide an item, and each flag records who and when ([Trail: flagging](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/1302671-flagging-checklist-items)). Maginhawa should keep **done, failed (done but out of range), couldn't do, and N/A** as four distinct states from day one.

## Temperatures, failures and offline use are where checks break

For temperatures, the leading pattern is a **Bluetooth probe that auto-records once the reading stabilises within range, with a numeric keypad as fallback**. Admins set per item whether a probe is allowed ([Jolt help](https://help.smartsense.co/en/articles/9654164-how-to-record-temperatures-using-the-temperature-probe-in-the-jolt-app)). Probes are fragile in practice. Crunchtime documents probes that fail to reconnect, and a reviewer complains that Bluetooth "some times is slow" ([Crunchtime support](https://support.crunchtime.com/hc/en-us/articles/15652909544467-Connecting-the-Bluetooth-pyrometer-to-Squadle); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/200103/Squadle/reviews/)). Manual entry must therefore always be one tap away. Since v1 has managers typing figures in, manual entry is the main path. Min/max limits are set per item in the builder, and an out-of-limits value prompts for an explanation and a **pick-list of preset corrective actions** ([FoodDocs](https://www.fooddocs.com/knowledge/how-to-create-food-core-temperature-log)). The input itself matters. GOV.UK advises against `type="number"` and says to omit `inputmode` where negatives are needed, because some keypads have no minus key ([GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/text-input/); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Global_attributes/inputmode)). Freezer readings are negative, so Maginhawa needs a **custom keypad with a dedicated minus key** or a ± toggle. The range should sit as a chip beside the field ("0–5 °C", "−18 °C or below"), and pass or fail should show the moment a value is entered. No vendor documents these last details. They are design inference.

Failure handling is the most consistent pattern in the category. A fail answer **triggers logic on the device**: an explanation, a corrective action, sometimes a required photo, and a follow-up task. SafetyCulture's triggers are ask questions, notify, require note, require media and require action ([SafetyCulture 000031](https://help.mitti.com/en-US/000031/)). Xenia shows the fullest flow. An offline walk-in reading over the limit triggers on-device questions ("what was found, what action was taken"), requires a photo at the moment of failure, and auto-creates a corrective task that appears after sync ([Xenia](https://www.xenia.team/audit/offline-audit-app)). SafetyCulture's sample temperature log includes a re-inspection time and temperature ([SafetyCulture library](https://public-library.safetyculture.io/products/daily-temperature-log-x7oajroeuqrikibx)). This supports a "**re-check in 30 minutes**" item that the failure adds to the same list, although no product documents a countdown UI for it. The panel should open inline beneath the item rather than as a modal, so the chef keeps their place. Raising a fault for maintenance maps naturally onto a FlowOutbox alert. That fits the optional Power Automate add-on in `HANDOFF.md` §3 and does not reopen any decision.

Offline tolerance separates good products from frustrating ones. SafetyCulture stores progress locally, but only for inspections already downloaded, and it tells users to **wait for the syncing progress bar before closing the app** ([SafetyCulture 000034](https://help.safetyculture.com/en-US/000034/)). That is a pain point to avoid. Xenia saves everything locally and keys records per person, so two staff offline at one site **sync as separate records and never overwrite each other** ([Xenia](https://www.xenia.team/audit/offline-audit-app)). Visa's design system and NN/g both prefer autosave to a Save button ([Visa](https://design.visa.com/patterns/wizard/usage/)). For Maginhawa this means saving each answer per item, never showing a blocking spinner on answer, and showing a quiet global state ("All saved" / "3 waiting to send" / "Offline – saved on this iPad"). Each answered row should show initials and time ("JR 07:42"), so a shared iPad shows who did what. Reopening a checklist should jump to the first unanswered item. Jolt's audible reminders and FoodDocs' push notifications show that products pull staff back when checks are due ([Jolt](https://www.jolt.com/?p=1963); [FoodDocs](https://www.fooddocs.com/haccp-app)).

## Template builders converge on draft, publish and "new runs only"

Template editing is a **desktop job in almost every ops product**. Jolt builds in its web portal and syncs to mobile ([Jolt: Edit Lists](https://help.smartsense.co/en/articles/9654543-how-to-use-the-edit-lists-interface-in-jolt)). FoodDocs only allows tasks to be created or edited on desktop ([FoodDocs](https://www.fooddocs.com/knowledge/how-to-create-a-monitoring-task-to-record-the-temperatures-for-storage-equipment-fridges/freezer)). Zenput's builder runs "in a web browser" ([Zenput docs](https://developer.zenput.com/docs/forms-endpoints-docs)). SafetyCulture alone offers full mobile editing ([SafetyCulture 000031](https://help.mitti.com/en-US/000031/)). The editor itself comes in three shapes. The first is a **structured list with rows that expand** to show type, limits, required and logic. SafetyCulture adds logic directly under the question it belongs to. The second is a **spreadsheet grid with bulk import** (Jolt). The third is a **document editor where "/" inserts a block** (Tally) ([Tally](https://tally.so/help/keyboard-shortcuts)). For restaurant managers, the structured list is the safest default. Trail and Lumiform both start managers from a **template library** ("Opening Checks", "Temperature records") rather than a blank page ([Trail](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/9088980-adapt-record-logs-and-task-templates); [Lumiform](https://help.lumiformapp.com/en/knowledge/template-builder-lumiform)). Every product found keeps Preview as a separate button. A live "what staff will see" phone preview beside the editor would be a differentiator, since no ops product was found doing it.

The drafts model is the clearest consensus in this research. SafetyCulture says edits "will be automatically saved as a draft. Your team won't get the latest changes until you publish" and shows an **"Unpublished changes" label with a revert option**. Published changes "only apply to new inspections moving forward" ([SafetyCulture 000031](https://help.safetyculture.com/000031)). Changes apply to scheduled occurrences only "as long as the assignees have not started" ([SafetyCulture schedules](https://help.safetyculture.com/en-US/using-safetyculture/schedules)). Typeform behaves the same way ([Typeform](https://help.typeform.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052109711)). Jotform's Revision History is the richest. It lists versions in time order with "Current Draft" at the top and a blue "Live" label, and **Restore turns an older version into the working draft** ([Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/help/how-to-restore-your-previous-published-version-from-revision-history)). Two traps show up as well. Trail's "Don't forget to hit 'Publish Changes'… to ensure any changes are saved" mixes up saving with going live ([Trail](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/9088980-adapt-record-logs-and-task-templates)). Jolt's "make schedule changes at least 24 hours ahead", needed because lists are generated a day in advance, is a technical side effect the user has to remember ([Jolt schedules](https://help.smartsense.co/en/articles/9654068-creating-and-managing-list-schedules-in-the-jolt-web-app)). Maginhawa should autosave drafts so that Publish only ever means "go live". Beside the button it should show a plain-English statement of the effect, for example "Applies from tomorrow's opening checks; lists already started today keep the old version". No product offers scheduled go-live, so that would be a new feature rather than an expected one.

Reordering uses drag handles everywhere (Jolt's two bars, Tally's ⋮⋮). However, **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 requires a non-drag alternative**, and automated tools cannot detect a missing one ([WCAG 2.5.7](https://w3c-wiki.pages.dev/wcag/2.2/aa/2.5.7)). The accessible recipe is visible Move up/Move down actions, plus keyboard pick-up with Space, move with the arrows, drop with Space, and an aria-live announcement ([GitHub engineering](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/exploring-the-challenges-in-creating-an-accessible-sortable-list-drag-and-drop/)). On phones, Apple's convention is an explicit Edit mode that reveals reorder and delete controls ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables)), so scrolling never drags a row by accident. Sections should be draggable blocks that carry their items with them.

Logic is answer-driven: "if [answer] → trigger". SafetyCulture enforces limits (40 nested fields per condition, and Skip to complete cannot combine with Ask questions) ([SafetyCulture 000031](https://help.mitti.com/en-US/000031/)). It attaches flags to response sets so they apply to every question using that set ([SafetyCulture 000111](https://help.safetyculture.com/000111)). Trail's model of "exceptions plus corrective actions" maps directly onto fridge checks ([Trail](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/9088980-adapt-record-logs-and-task-templates)). One caution concerns scores. SafetyCulture users note that large templates **hide failures inside a high percentage** ([SafetyCulture community](https://community.safetyculture.com/ideas/negative-templet-score-440)), so **flags beat percentage scores** for daily food-safety checks. Scheduling works best as **Opens / Due / Late until**. Jolt has a display time, due time and optional expiry ([Jolt](https://help.smartsense.co/en/articles/9654068-creating-and-managing-list-schedules-in-the-jolt-web-app)), and SafetyCulture has a completion window with a late-completion toggle ([SafetyCulture](https://help.safetyculture.com/en-US/using-safetyculture/schedules)). Following Notion's advice to check the next occurrence, the editor should add a confirmation line such as "Next: Tue 10 Nov, opens 07:00, due 09:00" ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/en-gb/help/guides/automate-work-repeating-database-templates)).

The head office versus site question is the least solved part of the category. Jolt uses **content groups with subscribing locations**. Central edits reach subscribers "about 5 minutes later", and editors must switch mode ("content group or location") before editing ([Jolt](https://help.smartsense.co/en/articles/9654543-how-to-use-the-edit-lists-interface-in-jolt)). Trail lets head office assign one task to many sites "for consistency" or to one site "that works differently" ([Trail multi-site](https://trailapp.com/why-trail/multi-site)). No product documents **a locked central core with local extra items**, or a site hiding one central item. Maginhawa should design it explicitly. Head-office items carry a "Head office" badge and are locked at site level. A "Site extras" section belongs to the site manager. A persistent "Editing as: Head office / Soho" scope switch follows Jolt's precedent. No vendor documents a pre-publish validation summary either. A "2 things to fix before publishing" panel is worth building and would be new. It would catch an item with no answer type, a range with no limits, a schedule with no due time, or a template assigned to no site.

## Corrections must add a new version, never overwrite the old one

UK food guidance says what records must contain, not how to fix them. The FSA's SFBB diary asks the manager to **sign every day** (delegation is allowed, but someone named must own it). It asks them to note "what happened and what you did" when anything goes wrong. A **4-weekly review** flags any serious problem or anything that "went wrong three times or more" ([SFBB diary refill, Bromley copy](https://www.bromley.gov.uk/downloads/file/1315/sfbb-diary-refill-one-week-per-page)). Scotland's CookSafe adds a weekly manager sign-and-date with a deviations and corrective-action column ([CookSafe record book](https://www.aberdeenshire.gov.uk/media/yskfqls4/cooksaferecordbook.pdf)). MyHACCP Principle 7 asks for monitoring, deviation, corrective-action and verification records "appropriate to the nature and size of the operation", with **no fixed retention period** ([FSA MyHACCP](https://myhaccp.food.gov.uk/help/guidance/principle-7-establish-documentation-and-record-keeping)). SFBB says to keep records until the next inspection, or longer if the officer asks. Enforcement shows what is at stake. Salford prosecuted a takeaway whose hot-holding and freezer logs were **completed in advance** ([Salford City Council](https://www.salford.gov.uk/your-council/news/news-archive/news-from-june-2023/takeaway-faked-hygiene-records/)), and an EHO found falsified records at an Aberdeen hotel ([The Caterer](https://thecaterer.com/news/hotel/aberdeen-doubletree-accused-of-falsifying-food-safety-records)). The design consequence is blunt: **the app must never let anyone choose the time a check was done**.

To fill the gap in food guidance, the MHRA's GXP Data Integrity Guidance (Rev 1, 2018) is the clearest UK statement of a correctable digital record ([MHRA](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/687246/MHRA_GxP_data_integrity_guide_March_edited_Final.pdf)). It is written for medicines, not restaurants, so treat it as a ceiling to borrow from rather than a legal duty. Its rules translate into UI almost line by line. Records must be **Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original and Accurate** (ALCOA+). Changes must be made "without obscuring or overwriting the original record", recording who, what, when and **why**. Users cannot switch the audit trail off. A scribe entry must identify **both the person who did the task and the person who recorded it**. Electronic sign-off must be linked to the entry it signs, and altering the entry must invalidate the signature. Review must include **a positive statement of whether issues were found**. Review may also work by **"exception reporting"**, which directly supports a head-office view that shows only what went wrong. Shared log-ins are "vulnerable to non-attributable data changes", which makes a quick per-person PIN on shared site iPads a compliance feature as well as a convenience.

Products show both good and weak patterns here. FoodDocs keeps statuses distinct: Done, **Missed** ("the scheduled time slot expired"), **Unscheduled** (added outside the slot) and Deviations. Missed tasks can carry an explanatory comment and stay visible rather than being "silently overwritten" ([FoodDocs](https://www.fooddocs.com/knowledge/timeslot)). Trail does the opposite in one important respect. Editing a record-log row **resets its timestamp to now**, and resubmitting moves the task time, so the original time is lost ([Trail timestamps](https://answers.trailapp.com/en/articles/4622658-how-can-i-see-when-a-task-checklist-item-or-record-log-entry-was-done)). SafetyCulture's inspection history lists who changed what, newest first, with relative times and exact times on hover, and exports to CSV ([SafetyCulture 000066](https://help.mitti.com/en-US/000066/)). However, its "Inspection started by" field actually shows the last editor, a labelling confusion to avoid. GitHub's **"edited" control that opens the revision list** is the cleanest row-level pattern found ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/moderating-comments-and-conversations/tracking-changes-in-a-comment)). Google Docs' filter for **showing only named versions** shows how to hide noise ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/google-docs-revision-history.md)). No product publicly documents a mandatory reason for change or strike-through of old values. Those come from paper good-practice rules and the MHRA, so here Maginhawa would be ahead of the vendors rather than copying them.

Put together, Maginhawa's model has two independent axes. A check has a **time status** (on time, late, missed, extra) and an **edit state** (original or corrected). A missed slot stays Missed with a required explanation. "Add late entry" records the real time now and displays "Late · 2h 10m after window". A "Correct this entry" sheet, not inline editing, asks for the new value, a required reason (quick picks such as typo, wrong unit, re-checked or entered for someone else, plus free text) and a corrective action if the value is out of range. It saves a new version. The row shows an **Edited** pill, and tapping opens a timeline with the old value struck through, the new value, the editor, the reason and the time. The original "Recorded" entry stays at the bottom. A sign-off marker sits in that timeline, and any later edit is flagged "after sign-off", which reopens the sign-off. Head office's day review then leads with a summary such as "38 of 41 on time · 2 late · 1 missed · 1 edited after submit · 1 out of range". It lists only the exceptions and closes with a sign-off that needs a positive statement ("No issues" or "Issues noted: …"). The SFBB "three times in four weeks" rule becomes an automatic repeat-problem flag per site and per check.

## The numbers for spacing, type and touch agree more than they differ

On touch targets, the standards stack neatly. **WCAG 2.2 AA requires 24 × 24 CSS px**, and W3C suggests **44 × 44 (AAA)** for important controls ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Apple's iOS default is **44 × 44 pt** with a 28 pt minimum, about **12 pt padding around bezelled controls**, and the line that "spacing is as important as size" ([Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json)). Material's 48 × 48 dp is widely cited, but its page could not be read directly and needs a check before it is quoted. NN/g puts the floor at **1 cm × 1 cm** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)). Hoober's field data shows that people hit the **centre of the screen** most accurately, and that targets at the top and bottom edges need **10–12 mm spacing** against 7 mm in the centre. Of the touches he observed, 49% were one-handed ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/11/design-for-fingers-and-thumbs-instead-of-touch.php); [UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)). Kitchens add gloves and wet fingers. A small military study found lower first-try accuracy with gloves, but **no measured wet-finger threshold exists** ([study record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........9465c900e5780072de7a168d106fd14d)). Pushing kitchen screens to 48–56 px rows is reasoning from the edge-zone data, not a measured result.

On spacing and density, Material's list rows are **56 / 72 / 88** for one, two and three lines ([Material Components List](https://gitea.com/784622644/material-components-android/src/branch/master/docs/components/List.md)). NN/g's proximity rule says a label must sit nearer its own field than the one before ([NN/g forms](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/web-form-design/)). A 4 px base with 8 px steps fits these numbers well. Use 4–12 px inside a component, 24–48 px between sections, and 16 px phone gutters. Monzo-style grouping then comes in three levels: whitespace first, inset rounded cards second, and inset hairlines only within a card. No first-party Monzo, Things or Linear spacing specs exist, and the third-party "Monzo design system" sites contradict each other ([example](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/monzo/)), so treat them as unreliable. Density should depend on the job. Use comfortable rows (56 px, one decision per row) for entry, and compact rows only on tablet review screens for head office and history.

On type, Apple's Dynamic Type sets **body at 17 pt, footnote at 13 and caption at 12/11, with an 11 pt floor**, and requires **4.5:1 contrast up to 17 pt and 3:1 above** ([Apple HIG Typography](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography.json)). Material 3's body large is 16/24 ([material-web tokens](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-web/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-typescale.scss)). A working scale for Maginhawa is 34 / 28 / 22 / 20 / 17 / 15 / 13. Hero figures sit at 34–40 px with **tabular numerals** ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric)), row titles at 17 px, and nothing a manager must glance at below 13 px. On tablets, cap content at about 600–680 px wide rather than stretching lists. Status must never rely on colour alone. Apple's own example pairs a green circle with a tick against a red octagon with an X ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json)). WCAG 1.4.1 and 1.4.11 require **3:1 for icons and borders** ([W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Muted grey text on the dark hero card is the likeliest place to fail 4.5:1. For forms, use a single column with labels above the fields, validate when the user leaves a field, clear an error on the keystroke that fixes it, show positive ticks, and keep an error summary on submit ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). Write errors in GOV.UK style ("Enter the fridge temperature", "must be 5 or less") ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/text-input/)). Cultured Code's only first-party view on motion is that animation should convey information and must not become tiresome when repeated ([sspai interview](https://sspai.com/post/39724)). For an audit record, a completed row should therefore **stay in place, dimmed**, rather than slide away as it does in Things.

## Principles and patterns to adopt

The table turns the evidence above into decisions. "Sourced" means a product or guideline does this. "Inferred" means the pattern is reasoned from the sources and needs testing with Maginhawa staff.

| # | Principle | Concrete pattern for Maginhawa | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | List first, any order | Checks grouped by Opening / Mid-shift / Closing. Answer in any order. Tapping an item opens a focused card with "Next unanswered" | Sourced (Jolt, Trail, Lumiform, NN/g) |
| 2 | One tap per routine answer | Full-row Things-style circle for ticks (22–26 px glyph, 56 px row hit area). Pass / Fail / N/A segmented buttons on the row | Inferred from HIG, Hoober |
| 3 | Four honest states | Done, Failed (needs action), Couldn't do (reason), N/A. Each shown with colour, shape and word | Sourced (Trail flags), WCAG 1.4.1 |
| 4 | Show what's left, not a bar | "4 left · 1 needs action" at the top. Outstanding list above Submit. Submit disabled with the reason | Inferred from GOV.UK, Baymard |
| 5 | Temperatures in context | Range chip by the field, custom keypad with a minus key, instant pass/fail. Probe-ready but manual-first | Sourced (Jolt, FoodDocs, GOV.UK); keypad inferred |
| 6 | Fail inline, act immediately | Inline panel: preset corrective actions, optional required photo, "Re-check in 30 min" item, optional FlowOutbox fault alert | Sourced (FoodDocs, Xenia, SafetyCulture); timer inferred |
| 7 | Save every answer, quietly | Per-item autosave, a global "All saved / Offline – saved on this iPad" state, no blocking spinners, resume at the first unanswered item | Sourced (Xenia, Visa, NN/g) |
| 8 | Draft, then publish | Autosaved draft, "Unpublished changes" banner, Publish, Discard, Revision history with Live and Restore | Sourced (SafetyCulture, Typeform, Jotform) |
| 9 | Say what Publish will change | "Applies from tomorrow's opening checks; lists already started keep the old version" | Sourced rule (SafetyCulture), wording inferred |
| 10 | Explicit editing scope | "Editing as: Head office / Soho" switch. Locked head-office items plus a "Site extras" section | Partly sourced (Jolt); inheritance inferred |
| 11 | Validate before publish | A "2 things to fix" panel: missing limits, no due time, no site assigned | Inferred (no vendor documents it) |
| 12 | Accessible reorder | Drag handle plus Move up/down in the ⋯ menu; Reorder mode on phones | Sourced (WCAG 2.5.7, Apple HIG) |
| 13 | Never overwrite, never backdate | "Correct this entry" sheet with a required reason. New version with the old value struck through. Late entries stamped now and labelled late | Sourced (MHRA §6.13, Salford case, FoodDocs) |
| 14 | Attribute every action | Per-person PIN on shared iPads. "Done by Ana · recorded by Ben" for scribe entries | Sourced (MHRA §5.2, §6.16) |
| 15 | Review by exception, sign with a statement | Head-office day summary lists only exceptions. Sign-off says "No issues" or "Issues noted". An edit after sign-off reopens it. Repeat-problem flag at 3 in 4 weeks | Sourced (MHRA §6.13/6.15, SFBB) |
| 16 | Comfortable for entry, compact for review | 4/8 px scale, 56 px rows, 17 px body, 13 px floor, tabular numerals, 4.5:1 text and 3:1 icons in both themes | Sourced (HIG, Material, WCAG) |

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that compliance and usability point the same way. Shoving a correction into a hidden field, letting staff pick a timestamp, or silently overwriting a missed slot is worse UX and also a legal liability, as the Salford prosecution shows. The patterns that make records trustworthy are the patterns that make the app calm to use: visible late labels, edits that keep the original, sign-offs that state what was found, and reviews by exception. The MHRA standard is stricter than UK food law asks. Adopting it costs little in a new build and gives head office a defensible answer when an EHO asks how a record was changed.

The open areas are also where Maginhawa can lead rather than follow. No vendor documents central-plus-local template inheritance, pre-publish validation, a mandatory reason for change, a live staff-view preview, or a re-check timer. Kitchen-specific evidence on gloves, wet fingers and glare does not exist. The next useful step is not more desk research but a short on-site test. Put a prototype of the list, the temperature keypad and the failure panel in front of chefs at one site during a real close, wearing gloves, and measure mis-taps and time per check.
