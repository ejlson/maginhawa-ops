How round 1 stood up

Four questions you asked, answered with measurements from the round 1 mockups and what the research found about other apps.

Are the sizes right?!Mostly, but the mockups lied

The phones were 300px wide inside; a real iPhone is 393pt. Everything looked about 25% more cramped than it would be. Body text was 15px; iOS uses 17.

Is it overwhelming?!In places

Rows had up to 5 parts (circle, name, detail, reading, pill). The hero took 33% of the screen. The fix panel had 8 chips and a toggle at once.

Is it easy to follow?✓Yes

A grouped list with one Submit is the industry pattern, and the footer said what was blocking. The weak spot: nothing pointed to “do this one next”.

Does it feel like a chore?✕Still a bit

Finished rows kept taking up space, there was no sense of momentum and no finish line. Round 2 adds “Up next”, folding of done items and a finish screen.

What makes checklists feel like a chore, and what we do about it

Hunting for the next thingEyes scan the whole list each time.
“Up next” card always shows the next unanswered check; entering a reading moves straight to the next one.
Done items clutter the screenThe list never gets shorter.
Done checks fold into one “3 done” row you can open. They stay on the record.
No sense of progressA count that changes by one.
Circles fill in, the bar grows, and a finish screen shows time taken and problems dealt with.
Too many choices when something failsPanic plus a wall of options.
Four likely actions, the re-check time pre-set, fault reporting one tap away.
Typing on a small field with wet handsMissed taps, wrong numbers.
48px fields, 56px keypad keys, a minus key for freezers, and the range shown before you type.
Tempting shortcuts“Mark all as Yes”.
Deliberately left out. Filling records in without checking led to a 2023 prosecution in Salford. Speed comes from fewer taps per check, not skipping checks.
Research principles from round 1
Routine checks belong in one list

All the products we checked use a grouped, scrolling list with one Submit. NN/g: step-by-step wizards suit novices and break down when people are interrupted.

NN/g · Trail, Jolt, FoodDocs
Deal with failure on the item

Preset actions, an optional photo, a raised fault. Trail won’t submit until it’s dealt with.

FoodDocs, SafetyCulture, Trail
Show the range before the reading

Pass rule next to the field; verdict the moment you type. Freezers need a minus key.

Jolt, FoodDocs · GOV.UK
44px targets, 12px apart

48px or more for main actions; centre-screen taps are most accurate.

WCAG 2.5.5/2.5.8 · Apple HIG · Hoober
Draft, then publish

Autosaved draft, a clear “Unpublished” label, explicit Publish; changes apply to runs not yet started.

SafetyCulture, Typeform, Jotform
Reorder without dragging too

WCAG 2.2 needs an alternative to drag, such as up/down buttons.

WCAG 2.5.7
Never overwrite a record

Keep the original; record who, when, why; changes reopen the sign-off.

MHRA data integrity · SFBB
Head office reviews exceptions

Late, missed, edited, out of range; 3+ times in 4 weeks counts as serious.

Recommendation, round 2

Each screen is independent, so mix and match.

Fill out

D, Up next as the default: least hunting, clear momentum, still a full list underneath. F, Finish line at the end. C on the kitchen iPad, B as “Guide me”. Keep A if you’d rather the hero stays a summary.

Failed check

A, Fix it in the row, simplified to 4 answers with the re-check pre-set.

Edit a list

A, Draft-first with B, Reorder on phones; D, Open in place if managers mostly make quick tweaks; C on iPad and desktop.

Edit a check

A for editing, B when adding.

Corrections

A + B for sites, C for head office.

Quick fixes for the current build

These fit whichever direction you pick.

  1. 1
    17px body, 13px captions.

    The app’s own design language already says 17px; the run screen should match.

  2. 2
    48px fields and answer buttons, 8px apart.

    Replace the separate ± button with a keypad that has a minus key.

  3. 3
    A circle on every row; drop “pass” pills.

    Colour and words only for problems. Dim rows that are done.

  4. 4
    Focus the next unanswered check automatically.

    After a reading is saved, move straight on.

  5. 5
    Shrink the fill-out hero

    to title, count and bar (about 120px), or make it the “Up next” card.

  6. 6
    Footer names what’s blocking.

    “Fix the freezer, then 4 left”.

  7. 7
    Show who answered and when

    under each answered row.
