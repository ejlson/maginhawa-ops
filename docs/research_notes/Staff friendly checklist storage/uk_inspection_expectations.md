# UK inspection, audit and due-diligence expectations for digital daily checklist records (England focus)

Researched 9 Nov 2026. Labels: **[Verified]** = primary/official source read this session; **[Council]** = local authority guidance (official but local); **[Community/vendor]** = trade body, training provider or software vendor (self-interested); **[Inference]** = my reasoning, not sourced. Older material is dated in-line.

## 1. SFBB and HACCP: what must be recorded, review cycle, electronic acceptance, retention, corrections

### Takeaway
The law requires HACCP-based procedures plus records "commensurate with the nature and size" of the business, kept "for an appropriate period"; it sets no fixed format or retention period. The FSA explicitly allows SFBB diary pages to be completed and stored electronically, and the SFBB model is daily diary entries plus opening/closing checks plus a 4-weekly review.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** Retained Reg. (EC) 852/2004 Art 5(1): FBOs "shall put in place, implement and maintain a permanent procedure or procedures based on the HACCP principles." Art 5(2)(g): "establishing documents and records commensurate with the nature and size of the food business to demonstrate the effective application" of the HACCP measures. Art 5(4): (a) "provide the competent authority with evidence of their compliance"; (b) procedure documents "up-to-date at all times"; (c) "retain any other documents and records for an appropriate period." Art 5(5) omitted from 31 Dec 2020 (EU Exit SI 2019/642). — [legislation.gov.uk, Reg 852/2004 Art 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/852/article/5)
- **[Verified]** FSA SFBB page (published 5 June 2025): you can "keep an electronic diary – download the diary pages onto your device, fill them in and store them electronically"; "Store all your completed diary pages safely until your next visit from a local authority food safety officer. This can be done electronically or with printed copies." Daily records are kept in the diary. No fixed retention period is stated; the only benchmark given is "until your next visit". — [GOV.UK, Safer food, better business](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/safer-food-better-business-sfbb/safer-food-better-business-sfbb)
- **[Verified]** The SFBB diary file is titled "diary and 4-weekly review" (the FSA page links "sfbb-diary-07-diary-and-4-weekly-review"). — [GOV.UK, SFBB](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/safer-food-better-business-sfbb/safer-food-better-business-sfbb)
- **[Council]** West Norfolk (April 2026 newsletter): the SFBB Management section "explains how the system should be monitored and recorded", including "the checks you need to carry out, how often they should be completed, and the records you must keep"; the diary "includes a 4-weekly review, to help ensure that the document remains up to date"; you "could keep an electronic completed copy of the main pack and print the daily diary sheets on paper"; officers "expect to see the entire SFBB pack, not just the diary pages"; it "must be fully completed, up to date, and organised. (It is not the food officer's role to put your pack in order for you.)"; "The completed SFBB must also be kept on site at all times & diary pages and opening and closing records should be completed daily"; "the chef took it home" is likened to "the dog ate my homework". Also recommends archiving older versions of the pack along with their completed diary pages to show how the system developed. — [West Norfolk BC, Special edition food and safety newsletter, April 2026](https://www.west-norfolk.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/9885/special_edition_food_and_safety_newsletter_-_april_2026.pdf)
- **[Community/vendor]** NCASS (9 Oct 2025): businesses are "not legally required to use SFBB" and may run a digital food safety management system instead. — [NCASS](https://www.ncass.org.uk/news/the-ncass-digital-food-safety-system/)
- **[Council]** Retention, conflicting/local: Perth & Kinross (Scotland) recommends keeping records "for a period of 3 months with the exception of training records which should be retained on a ongoing basis". — [Perth & Kinross FSMS](https://www.pkc.gov.uk/media/40147/Food-Safety-Management-System/pdf/FSMS_version_3). Dover DC keeps its *own* intervention records (inspection forms, complaints) for 6 years per the FSA Code of Practice (this is the regulator's retention, not the business's). — [Dover Port Health Retention Schedule](https://www.dover.gov.uk/Corporate-Information/Retention-Schedules/Port-Health-Retention-Schedule.pdf)
- **[Verified]** Prosecution window under the Food Safety Act 1990 s.34: no prosecution after "three years from the commission of the offence; or one year from its discovery by the prosecutor, whichever is the earlier" (E+W+S; page current to 5 Nov 2026). — [legislation.gov.uk, FSA 1990 s.34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/16/section/34)
- **[Verified-ish, secondary]** Personal-injury claims (illness counts as injury, s.38) have a 3-year limitation from injury or date of knowledge (s.11/s.14), and courts may extend under s.33. — [Penningtons Manches Cooper summary](https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/time-limits-for-bringing-a-personal-injury-claim-what-you-need-to-know/); [legislation.gov.uk s.11](https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/11/4)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** A defensible retention rule is: keep daily checklist records at least 3 years (covers the s.34 maximum prosecution window and the basic 3-year personal-injury limitation); many operators choose 6 years to align with general civil-claim (contract/tort) limitation and accounting records, but that 6-year figure was not verified for food records this session and has no food-specific basis. Training records: duration of employment + a period. The ICO requires the period to be justified (see §5), so state the reason in a retention schedule.
- **[Inference]** Since the law is outcome-based ("demonstrate effective application"), the storage design should prove: what was checked, the reading, who, when (server time, not just device time), what was done when out of limits, and that a manager reviewed it (daily sign-off + 4-weekly review record).
- **[Inference]** Corrections: no official source found on how to correct entries. Paper good practice (single strike-through, initialled, never obliterate) translates digitally into append-only records with a visible amendment history (original value, new value, who, when, reason).
- **[Inference]** "Kept on site" (West Norfolk) is a paper-era expectation; for a cloud system, the equivalent is that the site team can show the records on site, instantly, without the chef or ops manager being present, including offline/outage fallback.

### Gaps
- No FSA statement found on minimum retention for temperature logs. The FSA page says only "until your next visit".
- No official guidance found on corrections/amendments to electronic food records.
- The 4-weekly review's exact contents were not extracted from the FSA diary PDF (it covers reviewing problems, changes to menu/suppliers/equipment/staff and signing off; confirm against the PDF).
- Insurer-specific retention requirements: none found; this depends on the policy wording, so ask Maginhawa's broker.

## 2. Food Hygiene Rating Scheme: confidence in management and what EHOs ask to see

### Takeaway
"Confidence in management" (CIM) is one of three scored elements and is the one most driven by records: the top band (0) needs "Food safety management records appropriate and maintained" and an "Excellent track record". To get a 5, CIM must be no more than 5, and gaps in records push it to 10, which caps the rating at 4.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** FSA business guidance (updated 30 June 2026): officers assess food handling, physical condition, and food safety management ("processes, training, and systems to ensure good hygiene is maintained"), after which "the officer can then assess the level of confidence in standards being maintained in the future." The rating is "a snapshot ... at the time of inspection." — [GOV.UK, FHRS guidance for businesses](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-hygiene-rating-scheme-fhrs-guidance-for-businesses/food-hygiene-rating-scheme-fhrs-guidance-for-businesses)
- **[Community] (reproduces Code of Practice descriptors)** CIM score descriptors:
  - **0**: procedures in place; hazards "properly controlled, managed and reviewed"; "Food safety management records appropriate and maintained"; all staff supervised/trained; "Excellent track record".
  - **5**: same, but records "appropriate and are generally maintained"; "Good track record".
  - **10**: "Generally satisfactory food safety controls"; records "appropriate and are generally maintained but some deficiencies/gaps identified"; "Satisfactory track record".
  - **20**: documented procedures "inappropriate or inadequate or little or no progress made since the last intervention"; "Varying track record".
  - **30**: "No evidence of food safety management/documented procedures"; "Poor track record".
  — [High Speed Training FHRS scoring table (2025)](https://highspeedtraining.co.uk/hub/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/fhrs-scoring-table.pdf)
- **[Council]** Total score 0–15 with "no individual score greater than 5" = rating 5 (Derby, April 2018); Doncaster: to get a 5 you must score no more than 5 in each of the three areas. — [Derby City Council](https://www.derby.gov.uk/media/derbycitycouncil/contentassets/documents/tradingstandardsandenvironmentalhealth/foodsafety/food-rating-scoring-explained-april-2018.pdf); [Doncaster Council](https://www.doncaster.gov.uk/services/environmental/food-hygiene-rating-scheme)
- **[Verified, document exists]** The authoritative scoring rules are in the FSA FHRS Brand Standard (Revision 8; FSA file dated March 2025). — [FSA Brand Standard v8](https://www.food.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/document/Version%208%20Brand%20Standard%20-%20for%20FSA%20website%20March%202025%20.pdf)
- **[Community]** CIM max is 30 vs 25 for hygiene and structure; a secondary source says CIM 20 caps the rating at 1 and CIM 30 caps it at 0. — [summarised via NCASS FHRS guide](https://www.ncass.org.uk/wp-content/Guides/Food%20Hygiene%20Rating%20Scheme%20Guide.pdf) (cap claim not checked against the Brand Standard)
- **[Council]** Officers "expect to see the entire SFBB pack, not just the diary pages", fully completed and organised, with opening/closing records done daily. — [West Norfolk, April 2026](https://www.west-norfolk.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/9885/special_edition_food_and_safety_newsletter_-_april_2026.pdf)
- **[Community/vendor]** EHOs are "increasingly comfortable" with digital records; showing the EHO checks on your device during an inspection is fine; the NCASS app produces a date-range PDF report that can be "displayed on screen, printed or attached to an email", showing actions reported and resolved and evidence of weekly/monthly reviews. — [NCASS, 9 Oct 2025](https://www.ncass.org.uk/news/the-ncass-digital-food-safety-system/)
- **[Community]** Council-linked guidance lists what an EHO expects: HACCP plan, temperature records, cleaning schedule, training records, pest control records; good features are real-time, signed and dated entries with evidence of corrective action and internal checks. — [High Speed Training, due diligence](https://www.highspeedtraining.co.uk/hub/due-diligence-in-food-safety/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Rating 5 needs CIM no more than 5, so "generally maintained" records with few gaps are the bar; visible, explained gaps are better than silent ones (a "deficiency" an officer finds unprompted reads worse than one the business has already flagged and actioned).
- **[Inference]** Presentation should be: (1) a live on-screen view at the site, filterable by date range and check type, with out-of-range readings and their corrective actions highlighted; (2) a one-tap PDF export for any date range (typically last 4–12 weeks) that the officer can take away or have emailed. Officers commonly take copies, which is consistent with Art 5(4)(a)'s duty to "provide ... evidence".
- **[Inference]** The "track record" element rewards continuity: keeping history across inspection cycles (at least back to the previous inspection, which may be 1–3+ years ago depending on risk category) matters.

### Gaps
- No official FSA/LA statement found on whether officers accept tablet-only viewing or must receive copies; evidence is vendor/trade-body only. Officers' powers to inspect and copy records (Official Controls Regs / Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regs 2013) were not checked this session.
- The current Food Law Code of Practice (England) Annex 1 CIM text was not read directly; descriptors come from a training provider's reproduction.

## 3. Due diligence defence (Food Safety Act 1990 s.21): records, integrity, audit trails, missed checks

### Takeaway
s.21 requires the defendant to *prove* "all reasonable precautions" and "all due diligence"; in practice that means documented systems, records showing they were actually operated, and records of checks/reviews of the system. Records with integrity (contemporaneous, attributable, unalterable or with visible amendments) are the evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** s.21(1): "it shall ... be a defence for the person charged to prove that he took all reasonable precautions and exercised all due diligence to avoid the commission of the offence by himself or by a person under his control." s.21(5): where the defence blames another person, notice must be served "at least seven clear days before the hearing" (and within one month of first appearance). Extent E+W+S. — [legislation.gov.uk, FSA 1990 s.21](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/16/section/21). Note: one secondary source wrongly cites "two days" and some cite s.20; s.21 is correct. — [IHASCO](https://www.ihasco.co.uk/?p=13085)
- **[Council, 2015]** Nottinghamshire Trading Standards (Updated July 2015): "sitting back and doing nothing is unlikely to help you meet your defence"; steps scale to business size; "The system must be under the directing mind of the enterprise and the responsibilities of those concerned should be stated"; "The system should be written down ... training for staff should be given and recorded. Checks on the system should be also be recorded"; system "should be both pro-active and re-active"; complaints recorded and trends addressed; verbal supplier assurances are not adequate. — [Nottinghamshire CC due diligence leaflet](https://www.nottinghamshire.gov.uk/media/109053/foodduediligencebusinessinformation.pdf)
- **[Community]** The defence can only be practically demonstrated by producing bona fide records of implementation, more than verbal evidence; written records are considered essential where temperature control is at issue; "accurate consistent real time records"; and a system that reviews and audits its own operation, with written records of those reviews. — [High Speed Training](https://www.highspeedtraining.co.uk/hub/due-diligence-in-food-safety/); [The Caterer](https://www.thecaterer.com/articles/68053/due-diligence)
- **[Community/legal]** Case-law context exists (e.g. Walkers Snack Foods v Coventry CC, 1998) on the "reasonable precautions" test. — [CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/health-safety-see-also-water-pollution-walkers-snack-foods-limited-v-coventry-city-council-7-march-1998-queen-s-bench-divisional-court)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** For electronic records to carry weight, the design should give: server-side timestamps (plus device time); the authenticated user (not a shared tablet login with a typed name); no silent edits (append-only, or SharePoint version history kept and not purgeable by site staff); a record of *who reviewed* and when; and corrective actions linked to the failing reading.
- **[Inference]** Missed checks: record them explicitly as "missed" (system-generated at the deadline) with an optional reason, not as blanks. Back-filled entries must be shown as late (entered-at time vs check-due time). A record set that is 100% perfect with identical readings invites suspicion (a classic "pencil-whipping" red flag); honest gaps with actions are more credible. No official source states this; it is practitioner consensus.
- **[Inference]** Head-office oversight (cross-site dashboards, 4-weekly review sign-offs) is direct evidence of the "directing mind" and "checks on the system being recorded".
- **[Inference]** Because SharePoint list items can be edited by anyone with edit rights, the Netlify Functions back end should be the only writer (staff get read-only on the lists), and versioning should be on.

### Gaps
- No UK case found ruling specifically on the admissibility/weight of electronic temperature logs vs paper.
- No official guidance found on data-integrity standards (e.g. ALCOA-style) for food business records in the UK; that concept comes from pharma (MHRA GxP) and is applied only by analogy.

## 4. Primary Authority, local guidance on digital records, allergens, CIEH/FSA/LACORS

### Takeaway
Primary Authority (PA) partnerships are the formal route to get a digital system "assured" so all councils must respect it; several vendors/trade bodies use this. Nothing official says a business needs PA to go digital.

### Cited Findings
- **[Community]** NCASS directs EHOs with concerns about its app to NCASS and its PA partner, the Royal Borough of Greenwich, under the government-backed Primary Authority scheme. — [NCASS](https://www.ncass.org.uk/news/the-ncass-digital-food-safety-system/)
- **[Community/news]** Cambridge City Council offered, via a PA partnership, approval of a cloud food safety management system (Checkit) that other authorities could rely on. — [UKAuthority](https://ukauthority.com/articles/cambridge-oks-cloud-food-safety-system)
- **[Community/vendor]** The SFBB+ app claims it is "fully backed by EHOs through our Primary Authority partnership" (vendor claim). — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.sfbbplus)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** An 8-site group could seek its own PA partnership (usually with the home council of head office) and get "assured advice" on its digital checklist design and record format. That gives consistency across 8 London boroughs. This is optional and a cost/benefit call.
- **[Inference]** Natasha's Law (PPDS labelling, in force 1 Oct 2021) only matters if sites prepack food for direct sale (e.g. grab-and-go). Allergen *records* worth capturing: allergen matrix version/acknowledgement, supplier spec changes, staff allergen training. This was not researched in depth this session.

### Gaps
- No current CIEH or LACORS guidance on electronic records was found (LACORS was absorbed into LGA in 2010, so material is old).
- No FSA statement found specifically endorsing or setting standards for third-party digital FSMS apps.

## 5. UK GDPR: staff names, signatures, photos in checklists

### Takeaway
Staff identities in food-safety records are personal data. The lawful basis is clear (legal obligation/legitimate interests for food safety), but retention must be justified and limited, and photos and signature images should be minimised.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** ICO storage limitation: there are no fixed periods; you must be able to justify them; set a retention schedule with categories, purposes and periods, and review it; do not keep data indefinitely "just in case"; you may keep data to defend possible legal claims but delete it once a claim could no longer arise; anonymise when identification is no longer needed; pseudonymised data is still personal data. — [ICO, storage limitation](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/storage-limitation/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Store a staff ID/UPN reference on each entry rather than free-text names or drawn signatures; an authenticated Microsoft 365 login already is the "signature". Drawn-signature images add GDPR weight but little evidential value over an authenticated, timestamped action.
- **[Inference]** Photos (e.g. delivery labels, probe readings, clean-down) should be framed to exclude people; strip EXIF location; keep them on the same retention clock as the record.
- **[Inference]** Retention justification: "kept for 3 years (6 if adopted) to evidence due diligence and defend prosecutions/claims (FSA 1990 s.34; Limitation Act 1980)". After that, delete, or anonymise the staff field and keep aggregates.
- **[Inference]** Note in the staff privacy notice that checklist activity is recorded and may be shown to EHOs and insurers. Avoid repurposing check timestamps for performance or attendance monitoring without telling staff (Employment Rights Act 2025 context not researched here).

### Gaps
- No ICO guidance specific to food-safety records or signature capture was found.

## 6. Practical: how multi-site operators present records at inspection

### Takeaway
Evidence is mostly vendor/trade-body. The common pattern is on-device viewing at the site plus a generated PDF for a chosen date range, showing exceptions, corrective actions and review sign-offs. Councils still expect the whole system (procedures plus records) to be on site and organised.

### Cited Findings
- **[Community/vendor]** Date-range PDF report shown on screen, printed or emailed, including actions resolved and weekly/monthly review evidence. — [NCASS](https://www.ncass.org.uk/news/the-ncass-digital-food-safety-system/)
- **[Council]** Full pack on site, complete and organised; daily opening/closing records. — [West Norfolk, April 2026](https://www.west-norfolk.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/9885/special_edition_food_and_safety_newsletter_-_april_2026.pdf)
- **[Community/vendor]** Vendors claim electronic records are "accepted as valid evidence of due diligence" (no legal citation given; marketing). — [ASC, paper vs digital](https://ascfoodsafety.com/paper-vs-digital-food-safety-records-auditors/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Storage and presentation requirements for Maginhawa:
  1. **Site "Inspection mode" screen**: any manager can open it on the site tablet; it shows the food safety procedures (the SFBB-equivalent "safe methods") and the last N weeks of records, grouped by day, with exceptions first.
  2. **PDF export** per site and date range, with a cover summary (completion %, out-of-range count, actions closed, review sign-offs) and an immutable footer (generated-at, by whom, record count/hash).
  3. **Exceptions are first-class**: every out-of-range reading needs a corrective action and a re-check; missed checks are explicit.
  4. **Reviews are records**: daily manager sign-off; 4-weekly review per site (SFBB equivalent); head-office spot checks.
  5. **Offline fallback**: a printable blank sheet for outages, with a way to upload a photo of it later, flagged as such.
  6. **Version the procedures**: keep old procedure versions with the period they applied to (West Norfolk's archiving advice).
- **[Inference]** Scotland: Food Standards Scotland's CookSafe replaces SFBB, and FHIS (Pass / Improvement Required) replaces FHRS. Wales: FHRS display is mandatory (Food Hygiene Rating (Wales) Act 2013) and Wales has its own Code of Practice. These are background knowledge, not verified this session; they don't affect England-only sites.

### Gaps
- No independent EHO-written blog or case study on multi-site digital presentation was found in the time available; evidence is from trade bodies and vendors.
- Insurer/auditor (e.g. third-party brand-standard audits, insurer risk surveys) expectations were not found in public sources; ask the broker and any audit provider directly.
