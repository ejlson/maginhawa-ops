Maginhawa · food safety · brief for an environmental health officer

Checking our digital food-safety records before launch

Draft for discussion · prepared 27 October 2026 · all figures are our proposed settings, not records

Why we're asking. Maginhawa runs 9 restaurants in London. We are replacing paper food-safety diaries with our own phone and tablet app. Before staff rely on it, we want an officer to confirm that the rules it enforces are right, and that its records would count as evidence of due diligence (Food Safety Act 1990, s.21). This brief lists exactly what we'd like you to check.

1What the app records

Daily checks. Opening, closing, hot holding and fridge/freezer temperatures, with a time and the person's name on every answer. A failed reading must be fixed and re-checked.
Cooking and cooling. Core temperature for each batch cooked or reheated. A cooling timer starts when food comes off the heat.
Probes. Weekly calibration in iced and boiling water. A probe that fails can't be used for checks until it passes.
Allergens. The 14 allergens for each dish. Every version is kept, and staff confirm they've read each change.
Deliveries and recalls. Supplier (approved list only), chilled temperature, batch codes and use-by dates. FSA alerts are matched to the batches we received.
People. Hygiene certificates with expiry dates. A fit-to-work question at the start of each shift.
Complaints, inspections and notices, with deadlines for fixes and appeals.
Records you can trust. Finished checklists are locked. A supervisor can correct an answer only by giving a reason, and the old answer, who changed it and when stay visible. Times come from our server, not the device.

4Records and evidence: our questions

  1. Digital instead of paper. Would you accept these digital records in place of the SFBB diary pages? Is there anything from the diary that we're missing?
  2. Corrections. We never overwrite: a correction keeps the original answer, the reason, the person and the server time. Is that enough for a record to be trusted?
  3. The inspection pack. On a visit, a manager can print one pack for chosen dates: temperatures and checks, faults, allergens, cooking and cooling, probes, deliveries, training, complaints, and inspections. Health answers and customer details are left out. Is that what you'd want to see, and is reading it on screen acceptable?
  4. The written plan. The app records the checks, but it doesn't hold our HACCP-based food safety management system. Is a written SFBB pack for each site, kept alongside the app, the right approach?
  5. One answer for 9 sites. Our sites fall under more than one London council. Would you recommend a Primary Authority partnership, and could your council act as ours?

5What we'll bring

The app on a phone and an iPad, with example records. A printed sample inspection pack. Our draft SFBB pack. The list of approved suppliers.
